Privacy notice

Career Compass is a student prototype built at KCL FirstYearHack on 21 November 2026. This page describes what the app's code does today. It has not had a legal review.

What other people see

  • On the compass: only signed-in people whose app reports a position within 500 m of yours, and only while you are visible and your own app is reporting a position.
  • In the People tab: anyone you have invited, or who has invited you, keeps your name, your picture, what you do, your LinkedIn link and your tags in their list. That does not depend on distance, and it stays after you go invisible, sign out or decline.
  • Your name, your picture, what you do, your LinkedIn link, and what you offer and are looking for.
  • Your picture is shown on your card on the compass and next to your name in the People tab. It is the photo you picked on the profile form. If you picked none and signed in with LinkedIn, it is your LinkedIn picture, for as long as LinkedIn's link to it works. Otherwise it is a coloured tile with your initials.
  • The picture is loaded from an address that contains a random ID for you and does not ask for sign-in. Anyone who has been shown that address (everyone who has had you on their compass or in their People tab) can load your current picture from it later, including after you go invisible or sign out. It gives a face or initials, never a position or an email address.
  • A direction and a distance to you, measured from the position their app reports. The app never sends your coordinates to another user and shows no map, but a direction and a distance from a known spot are enough to work out where you are to within GPS accuracy, and the app does not check that the other person is really where their app says.
  • If you sign in with email, your email address is never shown to other users. The name box is pre-filled with the part of your address before the @; change it if you do not want others to see that.

If you sign in with LinkedIn

  • You are sent to LinkedIn to sign in. LinkedIn then tells the app its own ID for you, your name (full, given and family), your email address and whether LinkedIn has verified it, the link to your profile photo, and your language setting. LinkedIn may leave the email out.
  • LinkedIn does not tell the app your profile address or what you do. The LinkedIn link other people see is the one you type on the profile form, and the app does not check that it is yours.
  • The app reads this at the moment you sign in, and again only when you sign in again, never in the background. The access LinkedIn grants is used for that one read and is not kept. The app asks LinkedIn only for sign-in, your basic profile and your email: it does not ask to post for you, to read your connections or to send connection requests. “Connect on LinkedIn” only opens the other person's LinkedIn link.
  • The name box is pre-filled with your LinkedIn name; you can change it. Other users see that name and your picture. Your LinkedIn email address, LinkedIn's ID for you and your language setting are never shown to other users.
  • Signing out of Career Compass does not sign you out of LinkedIn. On a shared device, sign out at linkedin.com as well.

Why we use it

Only to point your compass at people nearby and let you invite them. We ask for your agreement before you start, and you can change your mind at any time. Your phone asks you separately before it shares your location; an iPhone also asks before sharing its motion sensors.

Of what LinkedIn tells it, the app today uses LinkedIn's ID for you (to recognise you when you sign in again), your name (to pre-fill the profile form) and the link to your photo (to show your picture), and it tells you in the People tab what it keeps. The rest (your email address, your language setting and LinkedIn's answer as received) is kept in the LinkedIn record for a possible later feature; no code uses it today.

Demo people

During demos the app can place made-up “demo people” around you. They are not real users and are marked as demo data in the database. Their pictures are always tiles with initials, never photos.

Your choices

  • Go invisible, or sign out, at any time: both buttons are under “Demo tools and what runs underneath” on the compass screen.
  • Refuse location access. The app never makes up a position for you: without one from your phone it reports none, you are on nobody's compass, nobody is on yours, and the page says why and what to do. If you had been sharing a position, the app asks the server to remove it. People you have exchanged invitations with still see you in their People tab. In demo mode the page then offers “Use a demo position instead”; only if you tap it does the app report a fixed demo position (Bush House, London) as yours and show your profile to people near that spot, including everyone else who chose it.
  • If you signed in with LinkedIn: see what the app keeps from LinkedIn, and delete it, in the People tab under “Your LinkedIn data”. “Delete my LinkedIn data” deletes the whole LinkedIn record described above: your LinkedIn email address, the link to your LinkedIn photo, your language setting, the name parts, LinkedIn's answer and the copies of LinkedIn's ID and of your profile address held in it. It does not delete your account, your profile in the app (including your name, which first came from LinkedIn, and the LinkedIn link you typed), a photo you picked, your invitations, or the sign-in ID that holds LinkedIn's ID for you. Your picture then becomes the photo you picked or your initials; a browser that has already loaded the old picture may go on showing it for up to 5 minutes. The record is made again the next time you sign in with LinkedIn. Like positions, deleted rows can be restored by Snowflake for a short period.
