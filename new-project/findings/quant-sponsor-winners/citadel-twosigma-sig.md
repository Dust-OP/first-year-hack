## Details

### 1. Pelagos: HackEurope 2026, Susquehanna Best Use of Data, 1st (lead-only)
- **Event and prize:** HackEurope, 21–22 Feb 2026, in Dublin, Paris and Stockholm. The prize list shows "Susquehanna: Best Use of Data — 1st Place € 7,000 in cash", plus 2nd (€2,000) and 3rd (€1,000) (verified-on-page, https://hackeurope.devpost.com/).
  - The Devpost page quotes no SIG challenge text.
  - Event judging criteria (verified-on-page): "Potential to become a startup… Technical complexity: We want to see projects that don't just have a shiny UI, but also use advanced tools & frameworks in the background… Execution… Is it a complete, well thought out package, or stitched up work by 10 AI agents?"
  - The field size is reported as 147 or 152 teams (lead-only; SIG and participant LinkedIn posts seen through a search summary).
- **Data:**
  - Live AIS vessel positions arrive over a WebSocket from AISStream (real-time).
  - Global Fishing Watch provides SAR dark-vessel detections and one-year tracks, pre-downloaded into SQLite because of rate limits (batch).
  - A CSV of flagged vessels gives the positive labels.
  - The team says it generated about 9,300 synthetic vessels "calibrated to published research" (verified-on-page).
  - The repo holds `backend/ml/data/real_events.csv`, about 186.8k rows (verified-in-source; counted with `wc -l`).
- **Processing:**
  - The model is positive-unlabeled (PU) bagging over random forests, using 16 engineered per-vessel features: AIS gap stats, distance to ship-to-ship transfer zones, flag of convenience, and similar.
  - Evaluation uses precision@k and recall@k.
  - Claude agents run in parallel for OSINT and sanctions checks, and a Reporter agent writes the PDF report.
  - Stack: FastAPI, asyncio, React, Mapbox GL, scikit-learn, Docker and Railway.
- **Code check:**
  - PU bagging is real. `backend/ml/model.py:22` defines `PUBaggingClassifier`, and `model.py:94` samples pseudo-negatives from the unlabeled set for each bag (verified-in-source).
  - Leakage-aware validation: `backend/ml/train.py:77` uses `GroupKFold` grouped by MMSI, so the same ship is never in both train and test. `train.py:147` warns "HIGH OVERFIT GAP" when train P@100 is far above test P@100 (verified-in-source).
  - Caveat: `backend/ml/load_real_data.py:9-13` sets label=1 "if vessel has strong heuristic signals (many detections + convenience flag + near suspicious zone + tanker/carrier)". Those signals overlap the model's input features, so the CV scores are probably optimistic (inferred).
  - `backend/ml/data_loader.py:55-57` silently falls back to synthetic data when no real data is present (verified-in-source).
  - CI (`.github/workflows/ci.yml:30`) only runs `py_compile` and the frontend build. There are no unit tests (verified-in-source).
  - The Devpost write-up admits a "placeholder trap": the model had silently fallen back to a hash on MMSI digits until they noticed.
- **Why it won (inferred):**
  - It used real-time, real-world market-adjacent data (oil, sanctions, insurance) instead of a toy dataset.
  - It named a hard statistical problem (no negative labels) and chose a principled fix (PU learning).
  - It showed honest evaluation (grouped CV, overfit gap) and a live map that makes the data concrete.

### 2. Folio: HackEurope 2026, Susquehanna Best Use of Data, 3rd
- **Event and prize:** as in #1. The 3rd-place claim comes from https://www.guust.com/ (verified-on-page). The same team also reports wins in the Gemini and Lovable tracks there.
- **Data:** the company's own unstructured data: Google Drive, OneDrive, Gmail, Outlook, PDFs and spreadsheets. It is crawled by agents and refreshed "at frequent intervals" (verified-on-page, Devpost).
- **Processing:**
  - A master agent coordinates four sub-agents: explorer, structurer, verifier and knowledge-writer.
  - Gemini handles multimodal extraction and Claude handles orchestration.
  - The output is a hierarchical knowledge base with contradiction detection and a human "verification questionnaire".
  - A D3 graph updates live from Convex.
- **Code check:**
  - Contradiction tracking is real. `agents/src/agents/master_agent.py:293-315` (`_tool_add_contradiction`) and `:675-678` collect contradictions from structurer reports (verified-in-source).
  - `docs/Pitch_notes.md:23` pitches the questions it asks a human: "Is this column a client code or an invoice reference? Which revenue figure is authoritative?" (verified-in-source).
- **Why it won (inferred):** it frames data quality (provenance, contradictions, a human in the loop) as the product. That matches a trading firm's concern with trustworthy data more than its use of statistics does.

### 3. DriveWise: PennApps XXV (2024), SIG Best Use of Statistics
- **Event and prize:** PennApps XXV, Sep 2024, UPenn. Challenge text (verified-on-page, https://pennapps-xxv.devpost.com/): "At Susquehanna, massive amounts of data drive our trading systems and other technologies every day. For this project track, we're looking for creative ways you can use statistics and data."
  - Judging criteria: "Released on Slack" (not public).
  - The project also won "Best Community Impact Hack (Sponsored by Perpay)".
- **Data:** "22 years of historical accident data" and "over 3.1 million historical accident data points", which the project maps onto the Philadelphia OSM drive network (verified-on-page). The source is not named. The data is batch, preprocessed at app start-up.
- **Processing:** a spatial join of accidents to road edges, turn-angle penalties, and a "skill level" parameter feeding a composite edge weight, then A*. It also has a RAG chatbot over the PA driver manual. Stack: Flask, osmnx, networkx, scikit-learn BallTree, React Native.
- **Code check:**
  - Accidents are snapped to the nearest road edge with a haversine BallTree. `DriveWiseFlaskBackend/app.py:44` builds the tree and `app.py:50` queries it (verified-in-source).
  - The composite weight is `base + accident_weight*count*(1-skill) + turn_penalty*turn_weight*skill` (`app.py:161`). The route is `nx.astar_path` (`app.py:181`).
  - Caveat 1: the Devpost text says "we modify the heuristic to account for accident density". In the code the heuristic is plain great-circle distance (`app.py:175-181`); the risk lives only in the edge weights.
  - Caveat 2: turn penalties *grow* with driver skill, which is the opposite of the stated intent (inferred from `app.py:161`).
  - Caveat 3: the input `lat_long.csv` (`app.py:16-17`) is not in the repo, a Python venv is committed, and there are no tests. It is not reproducible.
- **Why it won (inferred):** it took a large real dataset into a statistic users can act on (risk-weighted routing). It had a clear social-impact story and a "Mathematical Background" paper linked from Devpost. Its rigour was weaker than the pitch.

### 4. Study BUDD-E: PennApps XXIV (2023), SIG Best use of Statistics
- **Event and prize:** PennApps XXIV, Sep 2023. Prize text (verified-on-page, https://pennapps-xxiv.devpost.com/): "Best use of Statistics -- Sponsored by SIG… Winners will receive exclusive SIG swag!" Judging criteria: "To be announced".
- **Data:** a real-time camera feed on a Viam rover / Raspberry Pi. An action-recognition model (mmaction2) labels frames as reading, typing and so on. Study sessions are timed.
- **Processing:** per-session label counts are turned into percentages, and "focused" means one activity is above 70%. It shows "Study Stats", gives a candy reward, and answers questions via Whisper and GPT.
- **Code check:**
  - The "statistics" are a frequency count with a fixed threshold. `backend/main.py:66-104` counts labels and `main.py:94` has `if percentage > 0.7: focused = True` (verified-in-source).
  - `main.py` parses its input with `eval()` on model output (a security smell; verified-in-source).
  - The UI shows "You have been focused X% of the time" (`webapp/src/App.js:84`).
- **Why it won (inferred):** in a one-winner swag track, a polished hardware demo that turns sensor data into a visible stat was enough. The statistical depth was minimal. Treat it as evidence that SIG's PennApps track was judged loosely.

### 5. Citadel European Datathon 2024, 1st place (self-claimed)
- **Event and prize:** a Citadel / Citadel Securities regional datathon run by Correlation One. The report is dated 14 Apr 2024. Format and judging rubric are covered under "What the firms say they look for" below.
- **Data:** batch public datasets, several of them joined. Sources: FAO meat-consumption data, the CDC/FDA/USDA NARMS antimicrobial-resistance surveillance system, the US Census ACS, and USDA NASS/ARMS livestock data (report p.4).
- **Processing:**
  - Cleaning covered missing values (imputation or exclusion), outliers and harmonising codes. The datasets were joined on state, region and year. States were put into 10 regions because the CDC does not publish AMR data by state (report p.4–5).
  - Analysis used descriptive stats, time-series plots, correlation, and regression on pre-2007 vs post-2016 windows to control for the financial crisis.
  - Results include a 1-year lag between livestock antibiotic sales and human AMR, and r = −0.646 between median income and prescriptions per 1,000 people (report p.5–7).
  - The report is structured as Executive Summary → Technical Exposition → Results → Discussion, with Limitations and policy recommendations.
- **Code check:** the repo has only `README.md` and the PDF report, with no code (verified-in-source; file listing). The 1st-place claim is `README.md:2`. The report text says "Care was taken to maneuver around the confounding factor that is human antibiotic prescriptions" (p.5).
- **Why it won (inferred):**
  - It asked a precise, policy-relevant question and joined several official datasets.
  - It explicitly handled confounding (the recession, human prescriptions) and stated a lag effect.
  - It ended with actionable recommendations. That matches the Correlation One rubric below, even though the statistics are fairly simple (correlation and regression, no causal model).

### 6. Castle Challenge (OpenAI × Citadel Securities × Sequoia), Nov 2026, 1st (self-reported)
- **Event:** a qualifying strategy puzzle for the "No Limit" student bot tournament. It is a Colonel Blotto game: 100 soldiers over 5 castles worth 1–5 points. Entries were scored "Head to Head" (against every entry) and "Four-Way" (over 1,000,000 random groups). There were about 6,038 entries (verified-on-page, https://kinshu.co/castle-challenge).
- **Data:** the public leaderboard. The author saved 319 versions of it, and submitted probe entries with known allocations to measure the field.
- **Processing:**
  - Field modelling used a weighted crowd of about 290,000 random allocations, fitted over 300 passes to the leaderboard score distribution, zero shares and probe scores. An exponential tilt was found by bisection, with a uniform prior.
  - Resampling gave uncertainty ranges. The fitted model predicted 8 held-out entries within 0.009.
  - Local neighbour search found the final allocations: Head to Head 4-5-36-48-7 and Four-Way 3-41-54-1-1.
  - The work was executed with Claude Code as an agent.
- **Code check:** no repo was published (verified-on-page). Not checked.
- **Why it won (inferred):** it treated a game-theory puzzle as a measurement and estimation problem: probe, fit, validate on held-out entries, then optimise. That is close to how market makers think about a field of competitors.

### 8. The LevelSet Suite: TreeHacks 2020, Citadel Best Decision Making under Uncertainty
- **Event and prize:** TreeHacks 2020. The prize is "[Citadel] Best Decision Making under Uncertainty Hack", one electric scooter per member. TreeHacks judging criteria include "Creativity… Technical Complexity… Social Impact" (verified-on-page, https://treehacks-2020.devpost.com/).
- **Data:**
  - Real-time call audio from Twilio and a Raspberry Pi with Google VoiceKit, transcribed by Houndify.
  - Training data: a hate-speech CSV of about 27k rows plus 63 hand-written microaggressions.
- **Processing:** DistilBERT embeddings feed a classifier. It streams flags to an HR dashboard (Node, MongoDB, Azure).
- **Code check:**
  - Devpost says "BERT Transformer with an SVC to achieve 90% accuracy". The code imports SVC (`model/train_model.py:8`) but actually trains `CatBoostClassifier()` (`train_model.py:68`).
  - The 63 microaggressions are duplicated 10× (`MICRO_MULT = 10`, `train_model.py:18,53`) *before* `train_test_split` (`train_model.py:69`). Copies therefore land in both train and test, which inflates the reported accuracy (verified-in-source; the leakage conclusion is inferred).
- **Why it won (inferred):** a real-time pipeline that classifies under uncertainty, with a polished multi-device demo. Judges did not audit the evaluation.

- **2019 Data Open Championship** (https://correlation-one.com/blog/caltech-team-wins-100-000-at-2019-data-open-championship, verified-on-page): "Each team was evaluated based on their submission's technical rigor, investigative depth, creativity, and real-world impact."

## Patterns (inferred)
1. **Real, messy data beats synthetic data.** The winners above that used real-time or large real datasets (Pelagos, DriveWise, the datathon report) placed higher than map.it, which relied on synthetic data and was runner-up.
2. **Name a statistical problem and the fix for it.** Examples: PU learning for missing negatives, a confounder window for the recession, a probe-and-fit model of a competitive field. "We used ML" alone is weak.
3. **Honest evaluation is the differentiator, but hackathon judges rarely audit it.** The older Devpost winners (#8, #9) and even DriveWise make claims their code doesn't support: data leakage, no held-out test, or a heuristic that doesn't match the description. Firm-run datathons explicitly score "handled the data honestly". The safest strategy is to show a held-out metric and caveats on the slide.
4. **End with a decision.** Every winner closes on an actionable output: a safer route, a flagged tanker with a report, a policy recommendation, or a Blotto allocation.
5. **SIG is the most active sponsor of these three right now.** It ran a statistics track at PennApps (2023–24) and a €10k "Best Use of Data" prize at HackEurope 2026. Citadel sponsors IC Hack every year (data/financial-data prizes in 2022 and 2024; logo only in 2026) and runs its own datathons. Two Sigma has no recent Devpost prizes.
